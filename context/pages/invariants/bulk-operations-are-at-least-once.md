# Bulk operations are at-least-once

> A message the user selected is, after the operation, in exactly one of three states the result names: done, failed with its sequence number and reason, or possibly duplicated. It is never gone without a word. Rule H of root `CLAUDE.md`.

## Why this exists

Version 2.3.0 fixed dead-letter bulk delete and resubmit losing and duplicating messages: the whole selection was scanned while earlier messages sat under a PeekLock that expired (`CHANGELOG.md`, 2.3.0). The fix moved the Azure provider to per-batch cycles, receive then send then complete inside one cycle. This page is the rule that fix established, so the next change to these paths is judged against it.

## Scope

- **Applies to:** `DeleteMessagesAsync`, `ResubmitDeadLetterMessagesAsync`, `MoveMessagesAsync`, `PurgeMessagesAsync` and, since 2026-11-12, `CancelScheduledMessagesAsync` of `IMessagingProvider`, on the active queue, the dead-letter queue and the transfer dead-letter queue alike (one receiver helper serves the three, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CreateReceiver`), the controller actions that call them, and the Angular bulk flow that shows the result.
- **Does not cover:** exactly-once delivery. The broker does not offer it across two entities, and the UI states the caveat.

## Where it's enforced (Azure provider)

- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync` - a resubmit cycle sends its targets, in as many batches as their size needs (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#SendResubmitBatchAsync`); when a send throws, every message of that send and of the sends before it in the cycle is reported as a possible duplicate before the scan ends (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#SendOrReportDuplicatesAsync`), whatever the failure, a timeout after the broker accepted the batch included, except a refusal of a disabled entity, which sent nothing; only a target never handed to a send can be reported not found (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#SendTrackedAsync`); `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync` only then completes each source message (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CompleteResubmittedAsync`), and a failed complete is recorded per sequence number with a "may be duplicated on retry" text (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CompleteResubmittedAsync`), not retried by resending.
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync` - a move cycle sends first, through the same send and the same report of a send that throws, and `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync` completes after (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CompleteMovedAsync`); `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CompleteMovedAsync` a move whose complete fails after the send is reported with the "may be duplicated" text defined at `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveDuplicateError`.
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ScanSafetyTimeout` - a scan stops after a safety timeout, armed in each of the three loops (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync`); `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#NotFoundInScanWindow` it also stops after consecutive windows with no target (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`), and the targets left are reported as not found (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#NotFoundError`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync`).
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync` - messages received and not selected are abandoned so their locks are released (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync` for resubmit, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync` for move).
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync` - each cycle checks the token (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync` for resubmit, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync` for move, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DrainAllAsync` for the drain of a purge or a delete of everything); only the safety timeout is absorbed into a partial result, a cancellation by the caller propagates (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync`).
- The controllers pass `HttpContext.RequestAborted` into every batch action, through one private action per operation that the active, dead-letter and transfer dead-letter routes share: delete `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Api/Controllers/QueuesController.cs#DeleteBatch`, resubmit `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Api/Controllers/QueuesController.cs#ResubmitBatch`, move `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Api/Controllers/QueuesController.cs#MoveBatch`, purge `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Api/Controllers/QueuesController.cs#Purge`, cancel of scheduled messages `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Api/Controllers/QueuesController.cs#CancelScheduledMessages`.

### Cancel of scheduled messages (2026-11-12)

A cancel deletes messages, so it is held to the same rule.

- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelScheduledMessagesAsync` - the sequence numbers are validated before any broker call: 1 to 500, each positive, none repeated (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/EntityConfigurationValidator.cs#ValidateScheduledSequenceNumbers`).
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync` - one broker call cancels all of them when all are scheduled; only when the broker answers that one is not found (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync`) is each number cancelled on its own (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync`), so the result says which ones failed. At most 501 broker calls.
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync` - a number that fails carries one of three fixed sentences (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#EntityDisabledError`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#NotScheduledError`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelFailedError`), never the broker's text; any other failure of the first call fails every number (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync`). The causes are logged once per request (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelScheduledCoreAsync`).
- `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync` - the token is checked before each single-number call and handed to the SDK.

### A disabled entity (2026-11-12)

The broker refuses a send to, or a receive from, an entity disabled for that direction. The provider answers with the neutral `EntityDisabledException` (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Abstractions/EntityAdministrationExceptions.cs#EntityDisabledException`), which the controllers turn into 409 with a fixed text (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Api/Controllers/EntityAdministrationResults.cs#EntityDisabled`).

- **Before anything was done, the operation throws.** Delete `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`, resubmit `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync`, move `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync`, cancel `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync` and `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync`.
- **Found midway, it is reported per message and nothing is retried.** What was already deleted, sent or cancelled keeps its outcome; every remaining sequence number is a failure with the text "The entity is disabled for this operation." (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync`, written at `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#ResubmitCoreAsync`, `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#MoveCoreAsync`; cancel `src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/Azure/AzureServiceBusProvider.cs#CancelEachScheduledAsync`). The loop ends there.
- **What the exception guarantees, and what it does not.** It guarantees only that no message was removed from its source. When the broker refuses a later batch of a cycle, the batches before it were sent; they are reported as possible duplicates and the call answers its result, not the 409, which comes only when nothing was sent yet.

### The automatic dead-letter resubmit of the web app (2026-11-13)

The client decides which sequence numbers each resubmit-batch call carries; the server still sends first and completes after. A run goes on past a failure only where the API reads the dead-letter queue from a position, so it can move past what it leaves behind instead of reading it again ([[pages/services/web-app]]).

- **Bounded.** The run reads the dead-letter count N once, at its start, and peeks at most N messages in total, each counted whatever became of it (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#scan`, `web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#count`). The cursor only moves forward, past the highest sequence number of each batch, so a sequence number is never read twice by the run and no set of handled numbers is kept; a message enqueued at or after the start, or whose enqueued time cannot be read, is left alone, so a message the run resubmitted that comes back to the dead-letter queue is not sent again by it (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#from`, `web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#olderThanStart`). A message "Not found within scan window" is never passed by: the run waits the entity's lock duration, at most 5 minutes, and sends it once more (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#waitForLocks`); still not found, it is listed as failed and the run ends failed (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#parts`). Nothing was received or sent for it, so the second try cannot duplicate it.
- **Every failure per sequence number.** A failed message is recorded with its dead-letter reason and the API's error and left in the queue; a possible duplicate is recorded apart, never sent again by the run and never part of "Retry the N failed" (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#resubmit`, `web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#retry`). The report and its JSON file carry every one of them.
- **Unknown outcome is a possible duplicate.** A resubmit stopped while in flight, failed as a call, or answered with something unreadable, a failure entry included, may have sent part of its batch; every message of that batch is reported as a possible duplicate and kept out of the retry (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#unknownOutcome`). This over-reports on purpose: a message that was never sent is listed as possibly duplicated rather than silently retried. An error the run does not expect ends it failed (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#ResubmitRun`).
- **Stop cancels.** Stop unsubscribes the request in flight, which aborts the HTTP call, and the API passes `HttpContext.RequestAborted` on (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#call`). Before the web app posts a connect, a disconnect or a clear of all data, it stops every active run and waits until each has ended (`web-app/src/app/core/services/connection.service.ts#afterSwitchHook`, `web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#stopAll`), because the API swaps its broker inside that request and a failed save afterwards answers 500 with the new broker in place; until the API's status arrives no run starts (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#start`), and a failed request reads the status again (`web-app/src/app/core/services/connection.service.ts#catchError`). A status that arrives later stops runs too (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#ResubmitRunService`). The failed messages of a run from before are never retried under the new connection (`web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.ts#canRetry`). A stopped or failed run is never resumed.
- **Verified by** `web-app/src/app/core/services/resubmit-run.service.spec.ts#continue past a failure` (continue past a failure), `:126` (a possible duplicate is never resent), `:144` (a message that comes back), `:161` (never more than N, and an API that ignores the position), `:201` (pause, stop aborts the request, a change of connection), `:403` (report and retry without duplicates), `:447` (each failing call), `:495` (no retry under another connection), `:530` (runs stopped and ended before a connect or disconnect is posted, none started until the status after a failed connect), `:646` (a message not found is tried once more after the lock, never passed by), `:756` (the cursor past the highest number of a batch), `:780` (an unreadable enqueued time or failure entry) and `:806` (a possible duplicate is sent again neither by the second try after a message not found nor by "Retry the N failed").
- **What the exception guarantees, and what it does not.** It guarantees only that no message was removed from its source. A resubmit or a move whose first cycle was sent in several batches may have sent part of that cycle before the broker refused the next batch; those messages stay in the source, so a retry can duplicate them, and the 409 does not say so. This is the same hole as the partial send under Known exceptions, not a new one.

## Known exceptions

None is intended; each is recorded on [[pages/services/core]] with its citations and tracked in [[pages/roadmap/followups]].

- **The RabbitMQ provider does the opposite order, and loses messages three ways.** Its bulk delete, resubmit and move consume through the management API with `ack_requeue_false` (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.Core/Providers/RabbitMQ/RabbitMQProvider.cs#DeleteMessagesAsync`) and republish afterwards; it never reads the cancellation token. Each loss was reproduced against a broker in a container on 2026-11-12, and the test pins today's behaviour as a defect: a republish that reaches no queue is reported as success and the message is gone (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.IntegrationTests/RabbitMQProviderDefectTests.cs#Defect_move_to_a_missing_queue_reports_success_and_the_message_is_gone`); a response that fails to parse, as it does for a message published with no properties, throws after every message up to the highest selected position was already consumed, the unselected ones included (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.IntegrationTests/RabbitMQProviderDefectTests.cs#Defect_bulk_delete_of_messages_without_properties_throws_after_consuming_them_all`); a republish the broker refuses, as for another user's `user_id`, loses the message already consumed (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.IntegrationTests/RabbitMQProviderDefectTests.cs#Defect_resubmit_of_a_message_with_another_users_user_id_fails_after_consuming_it`). Until that is fixed this invariant does not hold for RabbitMQ at all. The decision recorded on [[pages/roadmap/code-audit-2026-11]] is to turn these operations off for RabbitMQ and rebuild them over AMQP.
- **The Azure provider loses a message on a resubmit or a move into an entity with duplicate detection.** The copy keeps the original `MessageId`; inside the detection window the broker accepts and drops it, the source is completed, and the result reports success. Reproduced on the emulator (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.IntegrationTests/AzureServiceBusProviderDefectTests.cs#Defect_resubmit_into_a_queue_with_duplicate_detection_reports_success_and_the_message_is_gone`).
- **A selective delete or move on an active Azure entity dead-letters messages the user did not select.** The scan abandons the non-targets, which raises their delivery count; with a low `MaxDeliveryCount` they reach the dead-letter queue while the result reports success. Reproduced on the emulator (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.IntegrationTests/AzureServiceBusProviderDefectTests.cs#Defect_selective_delete_on_an_active_queue_with_a_low_max_delivery_count_dead_letters_the_unselected_messages`).
- **A cancel that mixes in a number that is not scheduled cancels none on the emulator,** and every number is reported failed, where the unit tests expect only that number to fail (`src/ServiceBusExplorer.IntegrationTests/AzureServiceBusProviderDefectTests.cs#Defect_a_cancel_where_one_number_is_not_scheduled_cancels_none_on_the_emulator_and_fails_every_number`). Nothing is lost: the messages stay scheduled and the result says so. Real Azure was not verified.
- **Cancel of scheduled messages is refused by RabbitMQ,** which has none, and exists for queues only.
- **The single-message resubmit** sends first and returns silently when the original is not found again.
- **Single receive and single resubmit actions ignore the request token** ([[pages/services/api]]).
- **The Angular flow shows one final update, not per-batch progress,** and does not reload the list after a cancel ([[pages/services/web-app]]).

## Change protocol

A change that reorders receive, send and complete, or removes a failure report, needs a superseding ADR first. The `message-safety` review lens reviews every diff that touches these paths, on opus.
